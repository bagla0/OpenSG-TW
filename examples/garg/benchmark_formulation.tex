\section{Step 0 --- the problem statement (Pagano / Garg)}

A laminated strip, span $0 \le x \le a$ ($a = 1$~m), thickness $-h/2 \le z \le h/2$,
infinite across the width (cylindrical bending: nothing varies with $y$, $\varepsilon_{22}=0$),
simply supported, carrying a sinusoidal pressure on the top face only:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{33}\!\left(x, +\tfrac h2\right) = q_0 \sin(px), \qquad p = \pi/a, \qquad q_0 = 10^4~\text{Pa}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{33}\!\left(x,-\tfrac h2\right) = \sigma_{13}\!\left(x,\pm\tfrac h2\right) = 0,
\qquad w = \sigma_{11} = 0 \ \text{at}\ x = 0, a
\tag{Garg Eq.~21}
\end{equation}
with continuity of $(u, w, \sigma_{13}, \sigma_{33})$ at every ply interface (Garg Eq.~22).

\textbf{The three laminate families} (\code{garg\_layups.py}; total thickness $h = a/S$):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
case & stack & material & aspect ratios $S=a/h$\\
\midrule
A & $[0/90/0]$, equal thirds & Pagano: $E_1{=}25, E_2{=}E_3{=}1$~GPa, & 4, 10, 100\\
  & & $G_{12}{=}G_{13}{=}0.5$, $G_{23}{=}0.2$~GPa, $\nu{=}0.25$ &\\
B & $[0/90/90/0]$, quarters & AS4: $E_1{=}181, E_2{=}10.3$~GPa & 5, 10, 100\\
C & $[0/\text{core}/0]$, $0.1h/0.8h/0.1h$ & faces $E_1{=}131$~GPa; core $E{=}0.578$~GPa & 4, 10, 100\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Harmonic families and stations.}  Every field is one harmonic:
$\sigma_{11},\sigma_{22},\sigma_{33},w \sim \sin(px)$ (peak at $x=a/2$);
$\sigma_{13},u \sim \cos(px)$ (peak at $x=0$).  Hence the through-thickness comparisons:
$\sigma_{13}$ at $x=0$, $\sigma_{33}$ at $x=a/2$ --- the same stations Garg reports.

\textbf{Reference surface}: the laminate \emph{mid-surface} in every chain
(SG \code{fraction=0.5}; the exact solver's $z$ origin).

\section{Step 1 --- statics of the resultants (shared input; NO material property)}

Sectional equilibrium of a slice $[x, x+\mathrm dx]$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm dQ_1}{\mathrm dx} = -q(x), \qquad
\frac{\mathrm dM_{11}}{\mathrm dx} = Q_1(x)
\end{equation}
Integrating and applying the simple-support conditions $M_{11}(0)=M_{11}(a)=0$
(which force both integration constants to zero since $\sin \pi = 0$):
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;Q_1(x) = \frac{q_0}{p}\cos(px), \qquad M_{11}(x) = \frac{q_0}{p^2}\sin(px)\;}
\end{equation}
At the $\sigma_{13}$ station $x=0$: $Q_1 = q_0/p = 3183.10$~N/m (equal to the support
reaction), $M_{11} = 0$.  The strip is statically determinate --- this same $Q_1$ is
what an Abaqus plate solve returns (SF4 $=3182.44$, measured) and what integrating the
exact stresses gives ($3183.04$): one statics value, three routes.

\emph{Code}: \code{statics\_fsdt.py :: statics\_resultants(q0, a, x)} --- two lines
implementing the boxed result.  Called in \code{pagano\_bench.py::run\_case}:
\begin{verbatim}
Q1, _ = statics_resultants(q0, a, 0.0)     # x = 0 : Q1 = q0/p
FF_end = np.array([0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, Q1, 0])
\end{verbatim}

\section{Step 2 --- Chain I: MSG-RM}

\subsection*{2a.  The 1-D structure gene from the layup}
The plate SG is the wall discretised through its thickness: 5-noded (quartic) elements,
one per ply, nodes from $-h/2$ to $+h/2$ (mid-surface reference).  Quartic is the
degree that represents the whole warping ladder exactly per ply ($V_0$ quadratic,
$V_1$ cubic, $V_2$ quartic).
\emph{Code}: \code{opensg\_jax/fe\_jax/segment\_plate.py} (mesh generator);
layups defined in \code{examples/garg/garg\_layups.py}.

\subsection*{2b.  Homogenization: the 8$\times$8 plate law}
One call per laminate:
\begin{verbatim}
r = rm_plate_msg(thk, ang, mats, MATERIAL_DB, fraction=0.5)
\end{verbatim}
\emph{Code}: \code{opensg\_jax/fe\_jax/msg\_rm\_plate.py}.  Internally (equation
numbers = Yu 2003): assemble the SG matrices $E = \langle[\Gamma_h S]^TC[\Gamma_hS]\rangle$,
$D_{h\epsilon}, D_{hl_a}, D_{l_al_b}, D_{l_a\epsilon}$ (Eq.~30); solve the constrained
zeroth-order warping $EV_0 + D_{h\epsilon}\epsilon = H\psi\Lambda_0$ (Eq.~34, KKT) giving
the classical stiffness $A = D_{\epsilon\epsilon} + \hat V_0^TD_{h\epsilon}$ (Eq.~40);
solve the twelve first-order columns $EV_{1a} + \bar D_a = H\psi(\psi^T\bar D_a)$ with
$\bar D_a = (D_{hl_a}-D_{hl_a}^T)\hat V_0 - D_{l_a\epsilon}$ (Eqs.~43--45); project the
second-order energy onto the Reissner--Mindlin form by least squares over
$X = G^{-1}$ and Yu's 24 relaxed constants (Eqs.~56--61) $\rightarrow$ the transverse
shear stiffness $G$.  Returned: $\mathrm{ABDG} = [[A,B,0],[B,D,0],[0,0,G]]$ (8$\times$8)
plus the warping fields $V_0, V_{11}, V_{12}$ used by the recovery.

\subsection*{2c.  Resultants $\rightarrow$ plate strains (constitutive + one equilibrium step)}
\begin{equation}
\bm\epsilon = A_6^{-1}\,\mathrm{FF}[{:}6]
\qquad\text{(here } \bm\epsilon = 0\text{: only } Q_1 \text{ acts at } x{=}0\text{)}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\bm\epsilon_{,1} = A_6^{-1}\,[0,0,0,\,Q_1,\,0,0]^T\;}
\qquad\text{(equilibrium } M_{11,1} = Q_1\text{)}
\end{equation}
The closure was validated against the oracle gradient taken from the exact solution:
$\sigma_{13}$-profile difference $0.008\%$ ($S{=}10$) / $0.02\%$ ($S{=}4$).
\begin{verbatim}
S6      = np.linalg.inv(np.asarray(r["A6"]))
E6_end  = S6 @ FF_end[:6]
dE1_end = S6 @ np.array([0, 0, 0, Q1, 0, 0.0])
\end{verbatim}

\subsection*{2d.  Pointwise recovery (Yu Eqs.~63/66) and $\sigma_{33}$}
The implemented recovery is the full SECOND-order formula (Eq.~66), carrying the
$V_2$ warping blocks of Eq.~64 ($\bar V_{1a} = V_{1a} + \mathrm{kernel}\,c_a$ are the
relaxed first-order columns of Eq.~58):
\begin{align}
\Gamma(z) ={}& \Gamma_h S\big(V_0\bm\epsilon + \bar V_{11}\bm\epsilon_{,1}
 + \bar V_{12}\bm\epsilon_{,2}
 + V_{21}\bm\epsilon_{,11} + V_{22}\bm\epsilon_{,12} + V_{23}\bm\epsilon_{,22}\big)
 + \Gamma_\epsilon\bm\epsilon \notag\\
 &+ \Gamma_{l_1}S\big(V_0\bm\epsilon + \bar V_{11}\bm\epsilon_{,1}
 + \bar V_{12}\bm\epsilon_{,2}\big)_{,1}
 + \Gamma_{l_2}S\big(V_0\bm\epsilon + \bar V_{11}\bm\epsilon_{,1}
 + \bar V_{12}\bm\epsilon_{,2}\big)_{,2},
\qquad \sigma(z) = C(z)\,\Gamma(z)
\end{align}
For THIS load case the $V_2$ terms carry exactly zero, for two independent reasons:
(i) the constant-$Q$ statics state has $\bm\epsilon_{,11} = \bm\epsilon_{,12} =
\bm\epsilon_{,22} = 0$ (the second gradients of the resultants vanish when $Q$ is
constant), and (ii) for monoclinic laminates the $V_2$ contribution to
$\sigma_{13},\sigma_{23}$ is identically zero by the parity ladder regardless of the
load ($V_2$'s payload is $\sigma_{33}$, which enters here through the equilibrium
integration below).  The code nevertheless evaluates the full formula
(\code{msgrm\_strain\_at\_depth} accepts \code{dE11/dE12/dE22}).
The $\sigma_{13}$ content is carried by $\bar V_{11}\bm\epsilon_{,1}$; the
traction-free faces emerge naturally.  $\sigma_{33}$ by through-thickness equilibrium
of the recovered amplitude (families $\sigma_{13}\sim\cos \Rightarrow
\sigma_{33}\sim\sin$):
\begin{equation}
\hat\sigma_{33}(z) = \int_{-h/2}^{z} p\,\hat\sigma_{13}(z')\,\mathrm dz'
\qquad\text{(lands on } q_0 \text{ at the top face in every case)}
\end{equation}
\emph{$\sigma_{33}$: Yu's direct route, now implemented.}  Yu's 2003 recovery paper
states explicitly that ``it is not necessary to use integration through the thickness
of the 3-D equilibrium equations to get the transverse normal and transverse shear
\dots{} results presented herein'' --- his $\sigma_{33}$ comes from the 3-D
constitutive law applied to the recovered strain, which works because his
implementation carries the LOAD COLUMNS forced by the surface traction (his Eqs.~29
and 45: $V_1 = V_{11}\bm\epsilon_{,1} + V_{12}\bm\epsilon_{,2} + V_{1L}$).  The core
now implements them: $V_{1L}$ solves $E\,V_{1L} + L = H\psi(\psi^TL)$ with
$L = -S(\text{top})^Te_3$ per unit top-face pressure (one more right-hand side on the
same factorization), and the five second-order load columns $V_{2L}$ follow the same
collection that produced $\bar D_{2k}$, with $V_{1L}$ as the load-ladder rung:
$q_{,a}$ drivers $(D_{hl_a}{-}D_{hl_a}^T)V_{1L}$ and $q_{,ab}$ drivers
$-D_{l_al_b}V_{1L}$.  The recovery then takes the LOCAL pressure and its in-plane
derivatives ($q, q_{,a}, q_{,ab}$; here $q(a/2)=q_0$, $q_{,11}=-p^2q_0$) alongside the
strain drivers ($\bm\epsilon$ and $\bm\epsilon_{,11} = -p^2\bm\epsilon$ at the sin
peak), and $\sigma_{33} = [C\Gamma]_3$ DIRECTLY: both faces come out exact by
construction ($\sigma_{33} = 0$ bottom, $+q_0$ top, machine precision) and the profile
converges properly ($1.19\%$ at $S{=}10$, $0.012\%$ at $S{=}100$ for case A).  The
through-thickness equilibrium integration is retained in the \code{.dat} as a
cross-check column --- the two routes are asymptotically equivalent and agree to
$\sim 0.1$ points on the laminates (they differ visibly only on the very thick
sandwich, where the two truncations weight higher-order content differently).  In the
blade pipeline (beam resultants, no panel pressure) the load drivers vanish and the
direct recovery reduces to the self-equilibrated content, as before.
\begin{verbatim}
for i, z in enumerate(zc):
    s13_m[i] = msgrm_strain_at_depth(r, z, E6_end, dE1_end, z6)[1][4]
s33_m = cumtrapz(p * s13_m, zc)     # equilibrium integration
\end{verbatim}
\emph{Code}: recovery in \code{msg\_rm\_plate.py::msgrm\_strain\_at\_depth};
driver loop in \code{pagano\_bench.py::run\_case}.

\section{Step 3 --- Chain II: FSDT with the Whitney-1973 correction}

Input: the same statics $Q_1 = q_0/p$.  Whitney's construction (his equation numbers):

\textbf{(Eq.~1) The FSDT constitutive law.}
$Q_1 = k_1^2 A_{55}\,\gamma$ with $A_{55} = \int C_{55}(z)\,\mathrm dz$ and $\gamma$
\emph{constant} through the thickness (the FSDT kinematic assumption).

\textbf{(Eq.~3) The bending-stress gradient} under constant shear (classical lamination;
$\bar Q_{11}(z)$ = plane-stress reduced ply stiffness):
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\sigma_{11}}{\partial x}(z) = \bar Q_{11}(z)\,\frac{B_{11}-A_{11}z}{D}\,Q_1,
\qquad (A_{11},B_{11},D_{11}) = \!\int\! \bar Q_{11}(1,z,z^2)\,\mathrm dz,\quad
D = A_{11}D_{11}-B_{11}^2
\end{equation}

\textbf{(Eqs.~4--5) The equilibrium shear shape.}  Feeding Eq.~(3) into
$\sigma_{11,1}+\sigma_{13,3}=0$ and integrating from the free bottom face:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{13}(z) = \hat g(z)\,Q_1,\qquad
\hat g(z) = \frac1D\int_{-h/2}^{z}\bar Q_{11}\,(B_{11}-A_{11}z')\,\mathrm dz'
\end{equation}
Provable and \emph{asserted in the code}: $\hat g(\pm h/2) = 0$ (both faces free) and
$\int\hat g\,\mathrm dz = 1$ (the shape carries exactly $Q_1$; by parts,
$(A_{11}D_{11}-B_{11}^2)/D = 1$).

\textbf{(Eq.~7) The correction factor} --- equate the FSDT shear energy to the
complementary energy of that shape:
\begin{equation}
\frac{Q_1^2}{2k_1^2A_{55}} = \frac12\int\frac{[\hat g(z)Q_1]^2}{C_{55}(z)}\,\mathrm dz
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\boxed{\;k_1^2 = \Big(A_{55}\int \hat g^2/C_{55}\,\mathrm dz\Big)^{-1}\;}
\end{equation}
Self-checks in the module: homogeneous $\rightarrow k_1^2 = 5/6$ exactly (Reissner);
$[0/90/0]$ Pagano material $\rightarrow 0.5828$, the regime of Whitney's own 4-ply
example ($0.5952$).

\textbf{The standalone FSDT outputs} (what the $k$ is then used for):
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{13}(z) = C_{55}(z)\,\frac{Q_1}{k_1^2A_{55}} \quad\text{(layerwise-constant
staircase)},\qquad \sigma_{33}\equiv 0,\qquad
w\!\left(\tfrac a2\right) = \frac{q_0}{p^4D_{11}} + \frac{q_0}{p^2k_1^2A_{55}}
\end{equation}
Whitney uses the good shape $\hat g$ \emph{only} to calibrate the scalar $k$; the shape
is then discarded --- which is why no $k$ can repair the staircase profile, and why his
own validation (his Fig.~1) is deflection-only.

\emph{Code}: \code{statics\_fsdt.py} --- \code{whitney\_k1sq} (exact per-ply moments
$A_{11},B_{11},D_{11}$; cumulative integral for $\hat g$; the two assertions; Eq.~7) and
\code{fsdt\_s13} (the staircase).  FSDT does not touch Abaqus, Pagano, or the MSG core.

\section{Step 4 --- the reference: Pagano's exact solution (curves only)}

The per-ply 3-D equations reduce, for one harmonic, to a $6\times6$ state-space system
in $z$ with state $[u,v,w,\sigma_{13},\sigma_{23},\sigma_{33}]$, propagated by transfer
matrices with interface continuity and the face tractions of the problem statement
imposed exactly (boundary residual $\sim 10^{-15}$; resultant closure exact).
Equivalent to Pagano's $f_i(y)$ construction (Garg Eqs.~23--24).
\emph{Code}: \code{pagano\_exact.py} (entry point, families documented)
$\rightarrow$ \code{examples/TW-paper/rm\_thickness/exact\_cyl.py} (the solver).
The exact solution appears \emph{only} as the reference curves --- never as an input.
