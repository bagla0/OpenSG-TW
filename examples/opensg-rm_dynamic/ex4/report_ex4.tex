\section{Why this case}
Example 4 of Nayak, Shenoi \& Moy (\emph{Composite Structures} \textbf{64}
(2004) 249--267) is the one case in their paper with \emph{measured}
data: the natural frequencies of Crawley's cantilevered sandwich plates.
It answers a different question than the transient cases: not ``is the
time history right?'' but ``does the section law produce the right
\emph{stiffness-to-mass} ratios across many mode shapes?''  It also probes
a \emph{metal}-core sandwich --- shear-rigid, where every decent plate
theory should agree --- providing the control case against which the
foam-core Example~5 (where the theories split dramatically) is read.

All literature numbers are taken \emph{directly from their Table 5}: the
experiment, Crawley's own FEM, and Nayak's Reddy-HSDT finite element
(\textbf{9-node element, $8\times4$ mesh} --- his most converged column,
used as ``their'' result).  We compute nothing of theirs; the \emph{one}
new column is OpenSG-RM.

\section{Problem statement}
A CANTILEVERED rectangular sandwich plate --- clamped along the short edge
$x=0$, free on the other three edges:

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{ll}\toprule
planform & $a=152$ mm (cantilever length) $\times$ $b=76$ mm (width)\\
faces & eight graphite/epoxy plies total (four per side), nominal ply
$0.13$ mm\\
 & $E_L=128$, $E_T=11.0$, $G_{LT}=G_{13}=4.48$, $G_{23}=1.53$ GPa,
$\nu=0.25$, $\rho=1500$ kg/m$^3$\\
core & 1 mm sheet of 2024-T3 aluminium: $E=68.9$ GPa, $\nu=0.30$,
$\rho=2770$ kg/m$^3$\\
stacks & $(0_4/\mathrm{Al})_s$,\quad $(0/{\pm}45/90/\mathrm{Al})_s$,\quad
$((\pm45)_2/\mathrm{Al})_s$\\
wanted & the first five natural frequencies of each stack\\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}

\noindent\emph{Layup note}: the paper prints the third stack as
``$(\pm45/\mathrm{Al})_s$'' while stating \emph{eight} GE plies for every
plate; with 2 plies per face the frequencies come out $\approx$22\,\% low,
with 4 per face they land on Table 5 --- the stack is
$((\pm45)_2/\mathrm{Al})_s$.

\section{The OpenSG-RM route (the one new column)}
Per stack (\code{make\_abaqus\_freq.py}):
(1)~the layup becomes a through-thickness 1-D SG
(\code{ex4\_*\_sg.yaml}); (2)~\code{rm\_plate\_msg} homogenizes it to the
MSG $8\times8$ ABDG (the $\pm45$ stacks carry $D_{16}/D_{26}$ bending
anisotropy and a nonzero $G_{12}$ shear coupling --- all passed through);
(3)~a plain Abaqus S4 deck runs \code{*FREQUENCY}:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=deck]
*NODE / *ELEMENT, TYPE=S4                @\color{blue!60!black}\itshape 24 x 12 mesh (6.3 mm elements)@
...
*SHELL GENERAL SECTION, ELSET=EALL, DENSITY=4.33
...                                      @\color{blue!60!black}\itshape the MSG 6x6 (21 terms);@
                                         @\color{blue!60!black}\itshape DENSITY = rho*h = 4.33 kg/m2@
*TRANSVERSE SHEAR STIFFNESS              @\color{blue!60!black}\itshape the MSG shear block@
...
*BOUNDARY
NROOT, 1, 6                              @\color{blue!60!black}\itshape ENCASTRE the x=0 node row@
NALL, 6, 6                               @\color{blue!60!black}\itshape drilling dof fixed (flat plate)@
*STEP, NAME=MODES
*FREQUENCY                               @\color{blue!60!black}\itshape Lanczos eigensolver,@
8,                                       @\color{blue!60!black}\itshape 8 modes extracted, 5 reported@
*END STEP
\end{lstlisting}

Each job runs in $\approx$10 s; \code{collect\_freq.py} parses the
eigenvalue tables of the \code{.dat} files and assembles the table.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
(i)~Across all 15 modes and three stacks --- including the fully
anisotropic $\pm45$ ones --- \textbf{OpenSG-RM sits $-0.5$ to $-2.2$\,\% from
Crawley's FEM, statistically indistinguishable from Nayak's bespoke
9-node HSDT element at $-0.5$ to $-2.7$\,\%}.  The same accuracy is
reached with two section cards on a stock S4 element instead of a custom
higher-order finite element.
(ii)~Both theories share the same $+6$\,\% bias vs.\ the \emph{experiment}
on the first and third $(0_4/\mathrm{Al})_s$ modes --- a test-article
effect (root-clamp compliance), not a theory difference.
(iii)~On this \emph{metal}-core (shear-rigid) sandwich the theories agree,
exactly as they should; the discriminating case is the foam core of
Example 5.
