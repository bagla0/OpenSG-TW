# RESULTS — Nayak Ex.4/Ex.5 sandwich benchmarks: OpenSG-RM vs Abaqus 3-D solid vs Reddy HSDT

Everything in this folder, spelled out: **what** was computed, **where**, and
**why**. Layout: [ex5/](ex5/) = the transient sandwich (inputs, decks, Abaqus
results, Reddy scripts, all comparison figures); [ex4/](ex4/) = the Crawley
cantilever free-vibration case. Every figure is INDIVIDUAL, carries its own
legend, and is three-way (Abaqus 3-D solid = dashed black, OpenSG-RM = orange
with markers, Reddy HSDT = steel blue) wherever the theory provides the field.

## 1. The problem being solved (what and why)

**Benchmark source**: A.K. Nayak, R.A. Shenoi, S.S.J. Moy, *"Transient response
of composite sandwich plates"*, Composite Structures **64** (2004) 249–267.
They solve sandwich plates with a **Reddy third-order (HSDT) C⁰ finite
element** + Newmark time stepping. Their transient outputs stop at the center
*deflection* — no through-thickness stresses, because a smeared higher-order
theory has no credible interlaminar fields for a 9-layer soft-core section.
That gap is exactly what OpenSG-RM fills, so this benchmark lets us prove
**accuracy** (vs a 3-D solid), **reliability** (momentum-closure checks), and
**usage** (plain Abaqus S4 + post-processing) on their own problem.

**Example 5 — the transient sandwich** (all digits from the paper):

| item | value |
|---|---|
| plate | square, simply supported (SS-1), a/h = 10, h = 0.1524 m → a = b = 1.524 m |
| layup (bottom→top) | (0/90/0/90/core)s: 4 GE plies + PVC foam core + 4 GE plies; ply = 0.0125h = 1.905 mm, core = 0.9h = 137.16 mm |
| face material | graphite/epoxy: E_L = 128, E_T = 11.0, G_LT = G_13 = 4.48, G_23 = 1.53 GPa, nu = 0.25, rho = 1500 kg/m3 |
| core material | HEREX C70.130 PVC foam: E_c = 103.63 MPa, G_c = 50 MPa, nu = 0.32, rho = 130 kg/m3 |
| load | q(x,y,t) = q0 F(t) sin(pi x/a) sin(pi y/b), q0 = 68.9476 MPa; F(t): **step** (=1) and **blast** e^(-330t); their sine/triangular have t1 = 6 ms cut-offs |
| window | 20 ms, dt = 50 us (their accepted step) |

**Example 4 — free vibration**: Crawley's cantilevered GE/aluminium sandwich
plates (a = 152 mm x b = 76 mm, ply 0.13 mm, 1 mm 2024-T3 Al core; three
layups), with **experimental** frequencies — their Table 5.

## 2. The four Abaqus models (where they ran)

Generated by [ex5/make_abaqus_dyn.py](ex5/make_abaqus_dyn.py), run on the
remote Abaqus 2024 machine (RDP 10.165.18.74, commands in
[ABAQUS_RUN_COMMANDS.md](ABAQUS_RUN_COMMANDS.md)), results in
[ex5/Abaqus_results/](ex5/Abaqus_results/):

| deck | model | role | wallclock (cpus=4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ex5/sandwich_RM_step/blast.inp` | 20x20 **S4** shells wearing the OpenSG-RM section — the MSG ABDG **is** the Abaqus input: the 6x6 A/B/D block goes in as `*SHELL GENERAL SECTION` (DENSITY = rho h) and the MSG G block as `*TRANSVERSE SHEAR STIFFNESS`; no plies, no orientations, no correction factor | the OpenSG-RM route | **37–39 s**, fixed 400 x 50 us increments |
| `ex5/sandwich_SOLID_step/blast.inp` | 20x20x16 **C3D8I** solids: one element per face ply + 8 through the core, per-ply `*ORIENTATION` | the 3-D benchmark | **323–324 s**, automatic increments (~430) |

The S4 deck prints, every increment: center-node U (`NCEN`), and SF/SM +
COORD at three 2x2-element patches — `PATCHC` at the center (a/2, a/2),
`PATCHX` at the x-edge midspan (0, b/2), `PATCHY` at the y-edge midspan
(a/2, 0). These stations are where the double-sine solution puts,
respectively, the extreme bending/sigma33 and the extreme transverse shears
sigma13/sigma23. The solid prints center U every increment and centroidal S
in matching element columns (`COLC/COLX/COLY`) every 10th increment.

## 3. The OpenSG-RM chain (each step and why)

1. **[ex5/make_1dsg.py](ex5/make_1dsg.py)** — the entire 3-D layup enters as
   a 9-layer through-thickness 1-D SG ([ex5/sandwich_sg.yaml](ex5/sandwich_sg.yaml),
   5-noded quartic elements). MSG/VAM homogenization (`rm_plate_msg`) returns
   the RM plate 8x8: the classical 6x6 (= CLT for this layup) **plus the
   LS-identified 2x2 transverse-shear block G11 = 7.81 MN/m**. That shear
   number is the entire ballgame: the naive parallel integral of G(z) dz
   = 68.5 MN/m is **9x too stiff** because the soft core acts in *series*;
   MSG gets the series compliance with no correction factor.
2. **Abaqus runs the plate as plain S4** with that section — a standard 2-D
   shell analysis; nothing special in the FE model at all (the *usage* claim).
3. **[ex5/recover_dyn.py](ex5/recover_dyn.py)** — per time step: quadratic
   patch-fit of the printed SF/SM over the patch COORDs → resultants +
   in-plane gradients at each station → E6 = S6 R6 and its gradients →
   `msgrm_strain_at_depth` with the double-sine **load ladders** of the face
   pressure → all six 3-D stress components through the thickness.
   Two dynamic-specific pieces:
   - **sigma_a3 amplitude**: statically Q = M,x and the gradient-driven chain
     is complete; dynamically the thickness-shear ringing lives in the
     plate's *actual* resultant, so the SG through-thickness *distribution*
     is rescaled to carry the *measured* Q1/Q2 (SF4/SF5). Static limit → 1.
     Measured amplification here: **1.39** at the peaks.
   - **sigma33**: through-thickness integral of the *dynamic* momentum
     balance, sigma33,3 = rho u3_dd - sigma13,1 - sigma23,2 — the inertia
     term is ~40–100 % of q0 and cannot be dropped.

The Reddy comparison curves come from
[ex5/reddy_hsdt_navier.py](ex5/reddy_hsdt_navier.py): the exact single-mode
Navier solution of the TSDT (5x5 K/M, closed-form modal time integration) —
the mesh-free equivalent of Nayak's converged FE — plus its constitutive
through-thickness stresses (`reddy_profiles_*.dat`, `reddy_iface_*.dat`) and
the RM-Navier twin built on the same MSG 8x8.

| step | 2.690 m | **2.728 m (+1.4 %)** | 2.703 m (+0.5 %) | 2.292 m (**-14.8 %**) |
| blast | 1.834 m @ 2.451 ms | **1.854 m (+1.1 %) @ 2.450 ms** | 1.842 m (+0.4 %) | 1.603 m (**-12.6 %**) |

- `w_history_step/blast.png` — three-way deflection history: OpenSG-RM
  overlays the solid through 3.5 cycles; **Reddy HSDT is visibly stiff**
  (soft-core shear under-compliance) and fully out of phase by 20 ms.
- `profile_s13_*` / `profile_s23_*` — the transverse-shear paths: solid and
  RM agree on the ~157/230 MPa core plateau and face decays; the
  constitutive TSDT is a mid-core parabola that collapses to ~1/5 of the
  truth at the interfaces and explodes (30–90x jumps) inside the stiff faces
  (axis clamped, annotated). Reddy is drawn at ITS OWN peak instant — the
  fair envelope comparison, since its response is out of phase.
- `profile_s11_*` — three-way ply staircase at the center.
- `profile_s33_*` — solid vs RM equilibrium integral (TSDT has no sigma33).
- `iface_s13_*` — the design-driving face–core interface shear history:
  RM tracks the solid within ~5 % at every 50 us; the TSDT core-side value
  is ~5x low and out of phase.
- `reddy_fig13.png` — the Nayak Fig.-13 pulse-family reproduction (his
  w/0.0254 scaling) from the validated TSDT implementation.

## 5. Reliability checks (each one printable per time step)

- **sigma33 dynamic momentum closure**: recovered integral / applied q =
  **1.009 (step), 1.030 (blast)** — target 1.
- The Ex.2 anchor pins the TSDT implementation to Nayak's own converged
  9-node values (Table 3, 4x4, dt = 40 us) within 2–7 %:
  `TSDT Navier: 0.2011 0.6540 1.0225` vs `Nayak: 0.1870 0.6180 0.9983`
  (w/0.0254 m at 1.6/3.2/4.8 ms). His 4-node Table 2 is still shear-locked —
  do not anchor against it.
- The RM-Navier analytic twin (same 8x8, mesh-free) reproduces the Abaqus S4
  peak to 0.9 % — the S4 route carries no hidden FE artifacts.

## 6. Honest caveats (what the plate theory does NOT get)

- **sigma33 through the soft core**: correct end values and slope, but the
  solid shows a ~8–10 MPa thickness-stretch offset mid-core (the core
  compresses dynamically — a 3-D wave effect no 5-DOF plate kinematics
  represents).
- **Early time < ~0.3 ms**: the pressure wave takes ~0.15–0.2 ms to cross
  the 137 mm foam core; the solid's early response is genuinely 3-D.
- **Nayak's Figs. 12–13 cannot be compared quantitatively**: their sandwich
  figure shows period ~3.3 ms (~306 Hz) — close to a Voigt-parallel shear
  model (309 Hz), stiffer even than exact TSDT (193 Hz) — and an amplitude
  scale inconsistent with q0 = 68.9476 MPa by ~3 orders of magnitude
  regardless of the assumed axis unit. Their *tables* (Ex.2) are the
  quantitative anchor; the figure comparison is qualitative
  ([ex5/reddy_fig13.png](ex5/reddy_fig13.png)).

## 7. Ex.4 results — [ex4/](ex4/)

OpenSG-RM = the same 1-D SG 8x8 (yamls `ex4/ex4_*_sg.yaml`, generated by the
script) feeding a 5-field RM Ritz cantilever solve
([ex4/ex4_free_vibration.py](ex4/ex4_free_vibration.py)); no shear
correction, D16/D26 anisotropy included. Table:
[ex4/ex4_freq_table.dat](ex4/ex4_freq_table.dat); one comparison figure per
layup (`ex4/ex4_freq_04_Al.png`, `ex4_freq_0_pm45_90_Al.png`,
`ex4_freq_pm45x2_Al.png`), each Experiment vs Nayak HSDT FE vs OpenSG-RM:

- **(0_4/Al)s**: OpenSG-RM within -0.8…+6.7 % of experiment — and within
  0.2–0.5 % of Nayak's converged 9-node HSDT FE (both theories share the
  same +6 % bias vs experiment on modes 1/3: test clamp compliance).
- **(0/+-45/90/Al)s**: within +2.7…+6.5 % of experiment, <=0.7 % of Nayak.
- **((+-45)_2/Al)s**: within -0.8…+4.7 % of experiment, <=1.2 % of Nayak.
  (The paper prints "(+-45/Al)s" but states *eight* GE plies; 2 plies/face
  gives frequencies 22 % low, 4 plies/face lands on Table 5.)

So on a metal-core sandwich (shear-rigid, where HSDT is fine) RM = HSDT =
experiment; on the *foam*-core Ex.5 the theories split and the 3-D solid
sides with OpenSG-RM.
